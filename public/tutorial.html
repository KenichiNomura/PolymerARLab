Tutorial

Polymer AR Lab

Turn a hand-drawn Lewis structure — or a molecule loaded from PubChem or SMILES — into an interactive 3-D model, view it in augmented reality on the desk in front of you, build polymer chains, and export ready-to-run LAMMPS input for a quick educational relaxation.

🖥️ Desktop and 📱 mobile both work. On a phone you scan with the live camera; on a computer you upload a photo. Everything runs in your browser — nothing to install.

For education only. Polymer AR Lab is a teaching demonstration. Molecular recognition, generated polymer structures, UFF-style parameters, and simulation schedules are approximate and are not validated for production engineering, safety decisions, or research conclusions.

The interface at a glance

The 3-D molecule fills the screen. Controls sit in the corners: a row of tool icons at the top-left, the panel toggles and save button at the bottom-left, and the immersive-AR button at the top-right.

Top-left tool icons

CameraTurn the live camera on/off to scan a drawing (mainly for phones). Lights up when active. Once it's on, tap the on-screen frame to capture (drag its edges to resize, drag it to move).
Reset viewRecenter the camera on the molecule if you've orbited or zoomed away.
ClearRemove the current molecule and start from a blank scene.
AR ViewOn iPhone/iPad, open the molecule in AR Quick Look to place it on a real surface. Glows amber when the AR model is ready. (On desktop it downloads a .usdz file.)

Bottom-left panel toggles

EditOpen/close the molecule panel: load from PubChem or SMILES, upload a sketch, and toggle labels/hydrogens. Opening Polymer closes Edit, and vice versa.
Polymer builderOpen/close the polymer panel: choose the curing mechanism (addition or condensation), load your monomer(s), pick anchor atoms, and grow the chain (see Walkthrough ②). Opening it turns on atom labels automatically.
StatusShow/hide a small panel with the molecule summary (atoms, bonds, bond orders), the latest message, and a valence check.
TutorialOpen this tutorial page.
Save LAMMPS (UFF)Download the current structure as a LAMMPS data file + run script (see Walkthrough ②).

Inside the Edit panel

What each control does

Load — choose a source icon, enter the molecule, then press Load. The database icon selects PubChem names/CIDs; the smile icon selects SMILES. PubChem is selected initially.

SMILES is case-sensitive — uppercase C is aliphatic carbon, while lowercase c is aromatic carbon. For example, C=C(C=C)C is isoprene; lowercase c would describe aromatic atoms.

Upload sketch — pick a photo of a Lewis structure (best on desktop).

Atom labels — show each atom's element + index (C1, C2, O1…). You use these labels to pick polymer anchors.

Show hydrogens — display/hide H atoms (on by default; keep it on before saving LAMMPS files).

Building polymers lives in its own panel now — tap the chain icon in the dock (Walkthrough ②).

Inside the Polymer panel

How the builder works

Choose the mechanism first — Addition cure opens a C=C/C≡C directly or performs 1,4-addition on a conjugated diene. Condensation cure removes a small molecule such as H₂O or HCl. Choosing one starts from a clean scene.

Cis / Trans appears after the terminal atoms of an acyclic carbon 1,3-diene are selected. Changing the geometry after building resets to the monomer; press Make repeat unit again.

A / B slots — condensation polymers can use one self-condensing monomer (slot A only) or two different monomers. Tap a slot, then load a molecule into it; with both filled, they appear together labelled A and B.

Anchors follow the highlighted slot — the dropdowns always pick atoms of the active slot's monomer. Wrong picks show an error explaining what can react.

✕ Start over — back to the mechanism chooser (asks first if a chain is showing).

① Scan a hand-drawn Lewis structure & view it in AR

Draw a molecule on paper, let the app recognize it into 3-D, then stand it up on your desk in AR.

  1. 1
    Draw a Lewis structure on paper — clear bond lines and element letters recognize best (e.g. water, CO₂, ethanol).
  2. 2
    Capture it. 📱 Phone: tap the Camera icon, position the drawing inside the on-screen frame (drag its edges to resize), then tap the frame to capture. A shutter flash and a "Recognizing…" spinner show while it works. 🖥️ Desktop: tap Edit → Upload sketch and choose a photo. The app recognizes the drawing and builds the 3-D molecule.
  3. 3
    Explore. Drag to orbit, scroll/pinch to zoom, Reset view to recenter. Open Edit to toggle Atom labels and Show hydrogens; open Status to see atom/bond counts. Clear starts over.
  4. 4
    See it in AR — the molecule stands on your paper.
    📱 iPhone / iPad: tap the AR View icon (top-left, the cube). Wait for it to glow amber, tap again to open AR Quick Look, then aim at your paper and place it.
    🤖 Android: tap START AR (top-right), aim at your paper on the desk, and tap the screen — the molecule appears on that spot.
AR needs a rear camera and a secure (https://) page. If AR isn't available on your device, you'll still get the full interactive 3-D view.

② Load a monomer, build a polymer, export LAMMPS

Load a monomer from PubChem or SMILES, tell the app which atoms form the backbone, choose its geometry when available, and export a short educational LAMMPS relaxation.

  1. 1
    Open the Polymer builder. Tap the chain icon and choose Addition cure. The Edit panel closes and the scene starts clean.
  2. 2
    Load isoprene from SMILES. Tap the smile icon, enter C=C(C=C)C, and press Load. This equivalent ordering keeps the four diene-backbone atoms labelled C1–C4 in the app. SMILES is case-sensitive: use uppercase C here; lowercase c means aromatic carbon. Atom labels turn on automatically.
  3. 3
    Select the terminal diene anchors. Choose the first and fourth backbone carbons, C1 and C4. The app recognizes the conjugated diene and applies 1,4-addition: C1=C2–C3=C4 becomes –C1–C2=C3–C4–.
  4. 4
    Choose Cis or Trans, then build. Cis models natural rubber; Trans models gutta-percha. Press Make repeat unit, then adjust Repeats. If you change Cis/Trans afterward, the app returns to the monomer so the displayed chain cannot retain the old geometry—press Make repeat unit again.
  5. 5
    Compare, but do not over-interpret. Cis-1,4-polyisoprene is kinked and generally crystallizes less readily when unstretched; trans-1,4-polyisoprene packs and crystallizes more readily. Natural rubber can still crystallize under strain or suitable low-temperature conditions, so “cis never crystallizes” is not correct.
  6. 6
    Try condensation and PET. Start over and choose Condensation cure. Load ethylene glycol into slot A and select its two alcohol oxygens. Load terephthalic acid into slot B and select its two carboxyl carbons. The new ester bonds form between those oxygens and carbons.
  7. 7
    Understand the teaching model. In the simplified net esterification, the alcohol oxygen remains in the polyester linkage; the acid hydroxyl group and the alcohol hydrogen form H₂O. The floating water molecules visualize this net reaction and are excluded from AR and LAMMPS exports. Industrial PET production is more involved: initial direct esterification forms water, while later melt polycondensation commonly removes ethylene glycol.
  8. 8
    Export for LAMMPS. Press Save LAMMPS (UFF). Two files download:
    <name>.data — atoms, bonds, and a self-contained UFF-style force field (no external force-field file needed).
    in.relax — the run script.
  9. 9
    Relax it. Run lmp -in in.relax. The script does an overlap-relief warm-up → FIRE energy minimization → NVT at 300 K for 10 ps, and writes:
    <name>.xyz (one trajectory spanning the whole run) and <name>.relaxed.data (final structure).
Educational simulation only. Keep Show hydrogens on before saving. The generic UFF-style model and short, isolated-chain 10 ps run are useful for visualizing geometry relaxation, but they cannot establish bulk crystallinity or provide research-quality thermodynamics, kinetics, or material properties.

Tips & troubleshooting

Scanning

Good light and dark, clear pen strokes help. Recognition is a best guess — check the Status panel's summary and valence note, and redraw if it's off.

AR

Needs a rear camera and an https:// page. iPhone/iPad use the AR View icon (Quick Look); Android uses START AR (WebXR). Grant camera permission when asked.

Reset vs Clear

Reset view only recenters the camera. Clear removes the molecule entirely.

Status panel

Tap the Status (info) icon for atom/bond counts, bond-order breakdown, and a valence check that flags unusual structures.

Loading molecules

Select the database icon for a PubChem name/CID or the smile icon for SMILES. If SMILES fails, check atom case, parentheses, bond symbols, and ring closures.

What the simulation proves

The included LAMMPS run relaxes one finite chain in vacuum. Demonstrating crystallization requires multiple chains, periodic boundaries, careful equilibration, validated force fields, and structural analysis.

Polymer AR Lab is an educational demonstration and is not ready for production or research use.
